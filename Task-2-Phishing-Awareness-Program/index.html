01 — UNDERSTANDING PHISHING

What is phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which an attacker impersonates a trustworthy person, company or service to trick a victim into revealing sensitive information or performing an unsafe action.

✉

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to trick users into clicking malicious links, opening attachments or revealing personal information.

📱

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging applications.

☎

Vishing

Voice-based phishing where attackers use phone calls or voice messages to impersonate trusted organizations.

🎯

Spear Phishing

A targeted attack designed specifically for a particular person or organization.

02 — WARNING SIGNS

How to identify a suspicious message

01

Urgent language

Messages that demand immediate action such as "Your account will be deleted today."

02

Unknown sender

Check the complete sender address rather than relying only on the displayed name.

03

Suspicious links

Hover over links before clicking and verify the destination domain.

04

Unexpected attachments

Be cautious with unexpected files, especially executable or macro-enabled documents.

05

Requests for secrets

Legitimate organizations generally should not ask you to send passwords or authentication codes through email.

06

Unexpected payment requests

Verify unusual payment or account-change requests through an independent communication channel.

04 — SOCIAL ENGINEERING

How attackers manipulate people

Authority

The attacker pretends to be a manager, bank employee, government official or technical-support agent.

Fear

The victim is told that an account, payment or service will be suspended unless immediate action is taken.

Urgency

The attacker creates time pressure so the victim does not stop to verify the request.

Curiosity

Attackers may use interesting subjects, unexpected documents or sensational claims to encourage clicks.

05 — CASE STUDIES

Real-world phishing incidents

Google and Facebook phishing fraud

A well-known business email compromise case involved an attacker impersonating a vendor and sending fraudulent invoices. The campaign used social engineering and convincing business communications to persuade employees to transfer funds.

Security lesson: Payment or vendor changes should be independently verified using a trusted communication channel.

Fake password-reset messages

Password-reset themed phishing campaigns commonly imitate legitimate online services. Victims may be directed to a fake login page where credentials can be collected.

Security lesson: Instead of following an unexpected password-reset link, visit the service through its official website or app.

06 — PROTECTION

Practical security habits

01

Use multi-factor authentication. MFA provides additional protection if a password is compromised.

02

Verify unexpected requests. Contact the person or organization using a trusted channel.

03

Use strong, unique passwords. Never reuse important passwords across multiple services.

04

Keep software updated. Security updates help protect against known vulnerabilities.

05

Report suspicious messages. Use your organization's reporting process or the relevant service provider.

06

Slow down. When a message creates pressure, stop and verify before taking action.
